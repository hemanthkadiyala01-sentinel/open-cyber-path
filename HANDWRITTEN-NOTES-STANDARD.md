# OpenCyberPath handwritten-script notes standard

## Purpose

Write reference notes that a learner with no cybersecurity background can start using on the first day. The visual treatment should resemble handwritten study notes on ruled notebook paper, while keeping the text searchable, selectable, printable, and editable. These are digitally typeset handwritten-script notes, not scans of a person’s handwriting.

## Required learning structure

1. **Beginner:** everyday wording, define acronyms at first use, one idea at a time, relatable fictional examples, safety boundaries, and a short exercise.
2. **Intermediate:** connect concepts, interpret small synthetic records, compare plausible explanations, map to a framework when justified, and document evidence.
3. **Advanced:** detection quality, investigative reasoning, assumptions, uncertainty, control trade-offs, reporting, and a capstone-style case.

## Every attack-pattern page should answer

- What is the pattern in plain English?
- What is a fictional, non-operational example?
- What might a defender observe, and what does that clue not prove?
- Which prevention, detection, or recovery controls lower risk?
- What is a safe practice activity within synthetic data or an explicitly authorised lab?

## Safety and accuracy

- Never instruct learners to test random public systems, accounts, networks, or devices.
- Do not include deployable malware, credential theft, real-target exploit chains, destructive commands, or instructions to evade security tools.
- Keep examples synthetic; use reserved or clearly invalid example domains and fake accounts.
- Explain that indicators and alerts are clues, not automatic proof.
- Separate observed facts, possible explanations, unknowns, confidence, and next steps.
- Use official references such as NIST, CISA, MITRE ATT&CK, and OWASP; verify version-sensitive material before release.
- Include quizzes and answer checks but never promise a certification, job, or placement outcome.

## Publishing checklist

- [ ] Beginner, Intermediate, and Advanced sections are easy to find.
- [ ] Acronyms and technical terms are defined in ordinary language.
- [ ] The examples are fictional and non-operational.
- [ ] Every pattern includes defensive clues and mitigations.
- [ ] Exercises have answer guidance.
- [ ] References point to original publishers.
- [ ] PDF and editable DOCX are both available and render correctly.
- [ ] Each PDF page has been visually checked after rendering.
