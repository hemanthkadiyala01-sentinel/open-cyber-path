# Contributing to OpenCyberPath

Thank you for helping other learners find useful cybersecurity education. Contributions can be small: a verified link, a corrected cost label, a clearer explanation, or an improvement to a roadmap.

## What we welcome

- Official documentation and free learning resources.
- Open-source tools and projects relevant to cybersecurity learning.
- Hands-on labs with clear authorisation and safe-use boundaries.
- Career roadmap improvements based on real role requirements.
- Corrections to broken links, account requirements, pricing, accessibility, or hardware needs.
- Beginner-friendly explanations and reproducible project ideas.

## Before suggesting a resource

Please check the provider's own page and include:

1. Resource name and official URL.
2. Topics and skills it teaches.
3. Intended level and prerequisites.
4. Accurate access category: free learning, open source, free documentation, limited free tier, trial, or paid.
5. Whether an account, particular operating system, virtual machine, or paid cloud resource is required.
6. Date you checked these details.
7. Licence or attribution notes if you want us to include code, media, or other reusable material.

Do not describe an entire platform as free if only selected content is free. Never promise that a resource or roadmap guarantees employment.

## Safe and respectful contributions

- Recommend only legal, authorised training environments.
- Do not submit real credentials, personal information, private logs, or confidential employer data.
- Do not copy third-party lessons, lab walkthroughs, images, or datasets without permission.
- Avoid referral spam, paid-course promotion disguised as free learning, and misleading claims.
- Explain uncertainty rather than guessing.

## How to contribute

1. Open an issue with the resource or correction and its official source.
2. If you can edit the repository, submit a pull request with a concise description of what changed and how you verified it.
3. Be ready to update the entry if the provider changes its access conditions.

Maintainers may decline entries that cannot be verified, are unsafe, have unclear reuse rights, or are not useful to the project's learning goals.
