# GRC & Security Fundamentals roadmap

**Purpose:** Learn to describe cybersecurity risk, organise control outcomes, assign ownership, and track evidence-backed improvement work.

**Prerequisites:** No technical cybersecurity experience is required. Reading, writing, and spreadsheet-style organisation are useful.

## Modules

1. **Learn the language of risk** — define assets, business outcomes, threat events, vulnerabilities, likelihood, impact, and uncertainty. Write risk statements without implying an incident occurred unless evidence supports it.
2. **Understand frameworks and controls** — use NIST CSF 2.0 and CIS Controls as ways to organise outcomes and safeguards. A mapping is not proof of compliance.
3. **Build the risk register** — define the scoring scale, write a rationale, assign an owner role, choose a treatment, and set a review date.
4. **Collect evidence and review gaps** — distinguish policies from proof that a control is implemented and effective. Identify the document, configuration, log, or test that could support a claim.
5. **Communicate a decision** — summarise top risks, decisions needed, deadlines, dependencies, accepted risks, and assumptions for technical and non-technical stakeholders.

## Capstone

Complete the GRC Risk Register Starter workbook for a fictional service. Include assets, a transparent scoring scale, risk statements, control-to-evidence mapping, owners, treatment, and a one-page decision summary.

## Suggested resources and practice

- [NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework)
- [NIST CSF 2.0 quick-start guides](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework/quick-start-guides)
- [CIS Critical Security Controls](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls)
- OpenCyberPath: GRC risk register starter and cloud security posture review.

**Evidence standard:** A checklist alone does not establish compliance. Record the scope, control source, evidence reviewed, date, limitations, and owner for any real-world assessment.
