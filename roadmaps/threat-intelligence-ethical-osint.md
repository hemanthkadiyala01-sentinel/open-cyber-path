# Threat Intelligence & Ethical OSINT

**Level:** beginner to intermediate · **Primary focus:** carefully sourced, privacy-aware analysis of public information and threat indicators.

## Before you start

Practise web research, source citations, spreadsheets, basic networking terminology, and clear writing. Public availability does not automatically make personal-data collection ethical or harmless.

## Learning sequence

### Module 1 — Questions, scope, and privacy
Define the decision the research should support, the permitted sources, time range, and what data is unnecessary. Avoid targeting private people or compiling sensitive profiles.
**Practice:** narrow a fictional question about a company’s public security posture.
**Evidence:** a scope and privacy note.

### Module 2 — Source reliability and corroboration
Learn primary versus secondary sources, publication dates, provenance, context, confidence, and how to avoid treating a single claim as fact.
**Practice:** compare three supplied public-source excerpts and explain their strengths and limits.
**Evidence:** a source-evaluation table with links and dates.

### Module 3 — Indicators and context
Understand the role and limitations of domains, hashes, IP addresses, URLs, and MITRE ATT&CK techniques in investigations. Indicators change and can have benign explanations.
**Practice:** classify a supplied fictional indicator set and note what additional context is missing.
**Evidence:** an indicator table with source, timestamp, confidence, and caveat.

### Module 4 — Build an intelligence brief
Organise key judgements, supporting evidence, confidence, alternative explanations, and recommended defensive actions for the intended audience.
**Practice:** draft a one-page brief from a provided public incident report and synthetic indicators.
**Evidence:** sourced brief with assumptions and uncertainties.

### Module 5 — Capstone and review
**Capstone:** prepare a defensive threat-intelligence brief for a fictional organisation using public advisory sources and synthetic indicators. Do not probe, contact, or profile unrelated people or systems.
**Deliverables:** research question, source log, indicator assessment, short brief, and ethical/privacy review.

## Ready to move on when

You can trace claims to reliable sources, distinguish facts from inference, express confidence honestly, and propose defensive action without publishing unnecessary personal data.
